What it is

The only holder of your outbound keys.

Agents are eager and credentials are dangerous. Callisthenes keeps X, Reddit, and later email keys in encrypted custody, and lends the ability to send — never the keys themselves.

X first. Reddit next. Receipts always. Drafts never send.
Key positioning points

Why Callisthenes, in five lines

  • One custody point. Outbound credentials live here and nowhere else — not in your agents, not in your prompts.
  • Drafts never send. Nothing goes out without your word — or your standing order.
  • Permalink receipts. Every send returns the live URL. Evidence, not claims.
  • Throttle built in. Pacing and caps per tenant; one tenant's burst never starves another.
  • Any agent, one endpoint. Claude, Codex, Herald, your own — all send through the same guarded MCP.
The multi-tenant config

One container, all voices.

A customer is a row

One always-on Callisthenes serves every tenant on a single hostname. Your bearer token is your tenancy; keys, throttles, and usage are tenant-keyed rows.

Conformance by contract

Python, wrapping the upstream xmcp server — it meets the suite contract (tenants, billing, receipts) without a rewrite. Same guarantees, native stack.

Self-host = same image

The identical container with one tenant. Your keys stay on your box.

In the suite

The mouth of the operation.

1

An agent — routed by Ring, or a suite like Herald — drafts a post.

2

Callisthenes holds it as a draft: throttle checked, your approval or standing order required.

3

On your word it sends, returns the permalink receipt, and the receipt files back into Zenod memory.

Boundaries

What Callisthenes owns — and refuses.

  • Owns: outbound keys, send guardrails, pacing, permalink receipts.
  • Never: writes memory (Zenod), routes inbound (Ring), runs jobs (Epaminon), touches your channels (Phylax).
  • Never leaks keys. Agents get sends, not secrets.
