Zenod, the plan  ·  8 November 2026

Your memory gets helpers, an inbox and a line you can call.

Helpers read your memory while you sleep, leave what they find in one inbox, and you can talk to the whole thing by voice.

A plan for Jordi to approve before anything is built. Frames marked Live are the product today. Frames marked Coming are not built yet. Every mockup uses demo data.

1   What you asked for, in four pieces

Four new pieces, and the memory stays a memory.

  1. 1PersonasSmall helpers that wake on your memory, at night or when something new lands, and look for links, repeats and loose ends. Like dreaming. Coming
  2. 2InboxOne list of things that need you: a persona asks for a decision, or tells you what it found. Coming
  3. 3Voice noteA record button in the web chat. The note goes through the same filing as a WhatsApp voice note. Coming
  4. 4CallCall your memory. You talk to a fast hotline voice, and she asks the librarian for anything deep. Coming

What does not change: Zenod keeps no goals, no board of tasks and does not guess what you intend. Your WhatsApp chat keeps replying exactly as today. Everything a persona writes is a memory in your repo, marked with who wrote it.

2   Personas Coming

Helpers that read your memory while you sleep.

You switch a persona on, and it wakes on its own. You never have to ask it.

  • Night reader: once a night, reads what came in today and links it to older memories. It leaves a short morning note of what it found.
  • Librarian: spots two topics that are really one, or a memory filed in the wrong place, and asks you before it moves anything.
  • Loose ends: notices things you said you would do and never mentioned again, and tells you once.
  • Your own: later, a persona you describe in one paragraph, like a weekly look back at what mattered.

3   How a persona runs on your memory

Cheap, read-mostly, and only inside your own memory.

QuestionAnswer in the plan
What wakes itA nightly window, 02:00 to 06:00 in your time zone, the same window the weekly tree sweep uses today. Optionally when a new memory has been filed. A "wake now" button on its screen. If nothing new came in, it does not wake at all.
What it may doSearch and read your memory. Write only two things: a note under its own folder, marked as written by it, and an item in your inbox. It never edits or deletes what you said, never moves a memory without your yes, never messages anyone, never touches WhatsApp.
What it costsEach wake is one call to the cheap model Zenod already uses for answers. Reading a night's new memories and the older ones they touch, about 30,000 tokens in and 2,000 out, costs under one cent. Personas get a fixed share of the monthly AI allowance, one dollar of today's three, and stop for the month when it is spent. Each persona also has a cap per wake.
PrivacyA persona runs inside your own account on the Zenod server, with the same model provider your answers already use. It never sees another user's memory and nothing leaves the account. Switching it off stops it at once; its past notes stay, clearly marked, and can be deleted.
Many usersEach account has its own personas and its own queue. Wakes run on Zenod's existing job queue as one new kind of job, spread across the night so users do not all wake at once.

This is deliberately unlike Epic Team, where a persona is a full agent session on a server. A Zenod persona is one short model call, which is what makes it affordable at a few euros a month.

4   The inbox Coming

One place for what needs you.

  • Questions: a persona asks before it changes anything, with its recommendation first and two or three buttons.
  • Findings: something it noticed. You read it, open the memories behind it, or dismiss it.
  • Your answers are memories: each answer is written into your memory repo, so nothing lives only in a database.
  • Where you see it: in the web app, with a count on the tab. No WhatsApp messages from personas; that would change what your chat does, so it waits for your own word.

5   The voice note button Coming

Record in the browser, filed like a WhatsApp voice note.

  • What you do: tap Voice note, talk, tap again. You see the words appear as you speak.
  • What Zenod does: the recording goes into the same filing path as a WhatsApp voice note: kept word for word, the audio archived, filed in your tree, with the same receipt.
  • Ask or keep: the same rule as the one Send button, decided in its own epic. A question gets an answer; anything else is kept.
  • Cost: transcription with the model Zenod already uses, about 0.3 cents a minute.

6   Call your memory Coming

A library hotline that talks to the librarian.

  • How it works: the same design as the Epic Team desk call. A live voice in the browser answers at once; for anything deep she asks the librarian and reads you the answer when it comes.
  • Voice stack: OpenAI's live voice model, the small one, straight from the browser. Zenod hands the browser a key that lasts one minute, so the real key never leaves the server.
  • What she can do: search your memory, ask the librarian, keep something when you say "remember this", hang up. Nothing else.
  • Cost: about 2 to 3 cents a minute for short questions, up to 20 cents for long monologues, measured on the Epic Team desk. Calls stop at 10 minutes, and each account gets a monthly number of minutes.
  • What is kept: only what you ask to keep. The call itself is not saved as a memory.

7   Build it in Zenod, or merge with Epic Team

Build it in Zenod, copying Epic Team's screens and code.

Recommendation: do not merge now. Both apps are built the same way, so copying the pieces is cheap and leaves each product free to move.

PieceWhat we copy from Epic TeamWhat we write new for Zenod
CallThe browser voice engine and call panel, about 1,300 lines; the server's short-lived key, caps and price counting, about 1,000 lines.Her instructions and four tools pointed at Zenod's own search and answer; per-account minutes.
Voice noteThe record button and its segment recorder, about 900 lines.The upload into Zenod's existing voice note filing.
PersonasThe idea of a persona as a short file with its job written in plain words, and the wake on a timer.The runner itself: one model call per wake on Zenod's job queue, with the allowance share.
InboxThe shape of a question: recommendation first, two or three buttons, an answer that is written back to the repo.A small inbox screen and list. Epic Team's inbox is 8,000 lines tied to goals and reviews; none of that is copied.

Why not merge: Epic Team is about 108,000 lines built around goals, epics and agent sessions on servers, for one team. Zenod is a paid app for many people with its own sign-in, billing and deploys. Merging would drag the goal machinery into a memory product. Revisit when the names are final and the same voice code has run in both for a month; then the shared part can become one package both use.

8   What stays separate

Filing stays Zenod's, goals stay Epic Team's.

  1. Zenod keepsVoice note filingWhatsApp and web voice notes go through Zenod's own path: transcribe, keep word for word, archive the audio, file in the tree. Personas only read what was filed; they never sit inside the filing.
  2. Epic Team keepsGoals and the boardEpics, milestones, review cards, asks about work, agents on servers. None of it comes to Zenod. A Zenod persona has no goal, only a job: read and notice.
  3. The seam staysTwo clean wiresEpic Team reads a memory through Zenod's tools, and Zenod tells it "a new voice note landed". Copied code becomes Zenod's own code; nothing calls across at run time.

9   Does Zenod rest on EpicSpine?

No. They share one idea, not one system.

Zenod rests on your memory repo. EpicSpine is how the team runs work.

  • The idea they share: plain files in a git repo are the truth, and every database is only a copy. A Zenod memory repo is to your life what a spine is to a project.
  • Where they differ: a spine is a tree of goals with owners, steps and proof. A memory repo is a dated log and a tree of topics, with no goals at all. Putting a spine under Zenod would bring the productivity shape you said Zenod should not have.
  • What personas borrow: three small habits from EpicSpine's agents: wake, do one job, report; ask with a recommendation first; write the answer back to the repo. That is a pattern, not a dependency.
  • Where EpicSpine is used: building Zenod. This plan, its steps and its approval live in Zenod's spine, as always.

10   Where the line with Epic Team now sits

You asked for personas, an inbox and calls. Nothing more crosses.

Today's rule says Zenod gets none of Epic Team's machinery unless you ask for it in your own words. Your voice note of 8 November asks for exactly three things, so the rule moves by exactly those three and is rewritten that way once you approve.

Now allowed in Zenod

  • Personas that read your memory and write marked notes and inbox items.
  • An inbox of questions and findings, in the web app.
  • A voice note button and a call button in the web chat.
  • Copying Epic Team code for these, once, into Zenod.

Still not, unless you say so

  • Goals, epics, milestones, a board of tasks, guessing intent.
  • Commands or new replies in WhatsApp or Telegram chats.
  • Personas messaging you or anyone outside the web app.
  • Zenod calling Epic Team, or sharing its code at run time.
