The belief

Better context → better decisions.
And your context is mineable.

  • Invest in your context, own your context — to the exact extent it's mineable, it's worth cultivating.
  • Ten agents, one context, zero repeating yourself. That's not convenience — that's compounding.
North star: maximize how much of what you know and do today makes tomorrow cheaper — under your own keys.
The problem

Today, you are the context bus.

You re-teach every agent, carry context between them, triage every ping. Your context never compounds — it evaporates at the end of every session.

The move

Your context lives in N isolated pieces.
Centralize it — and give it an owner.

  • Centralizing means appointing ownership. A pile needs no owner; an asset does.
  • The evolution: distributed context, unowned → centralized context, managed by an agent. That's the whole move.
The core insight

Some dimensions of your life need exactly one owner.

Three scribes, no library

Let three agents write into your memory and within a week it's a junk drawer: three filing styles, duplicate facts, no provenance. A library needs one librarian.

Three voices, no style

Let three harnesses post on your behalf and you have no voice at all: three tones, three vocabularies, contradicting takes. A public voice needs one herald.

Wherever consistency is the value — your memory, your voice, your plan, your attention — appoint a single owner. Train him once. Everyone else goes through him.
What's new

Gatekeepers don't say no.
They keep order.

Train one governor how you like your posts. Then any agent — even the other governors — posts through him. That's not a veto. That's consistency, and if you're building context, consistency is paramount.

One door

Who are you talking to?
The Council.

They're not all listening in a room. The Council is the front door — it routes your words to the governor who owns them, and answers back through the guard. You never route anything yourself.

The powerful idea

The Council is itself an MCP server.

Any harness you'll ever use can add to your memory, mine it back, and — when it's stuck — reach you on WhatsApp through the guard. Authenticated, trained on you, gated. It never gets the keys; it gets an audience.

The integration, live

When work breaks, the Council talks it out.

  • Evidence passes hand to hand. Every message carries receipts, so every response is grounded in what actually happened.
  • Not just shared memory — a common language. That's the benefit of an integrated suite over ten disconnected tools.
Philosophy

The Council are GitHub maximalists.

  • No proprietary database. The substrate is repos you already own. Leave anytime; take everything, with history.
  • Human and agent writing always distinguishable. Provenance is first-class; discipline is code, not willpower.
Principles

Five rules the whole suite obeys

Everybody reads. One writes. Each domain has exactly one gatekeeper with write authority. Agents ask; they never get raw CRUD.
Discipline as code, not willpower. Schemas, filing rules, and state machines are enforced deterministically — not in prompts.
You hold the keys. Plain markdown, your git, your server, your API keys. Export with zero loss.
Auditable to the commit. Every memory, ticket, and send is a commit or labeled event.
Outward needs your word — or your standing order. Approve live, or pre-approve and let it flow.
Architecture

Run it your way.
The vault stays in your GitHub either way.

Leave the hosted service, spin up the container, point it at the same repo — nothing to migrate. The vault never lived with us in the first place.

What we say when they ask

The answers, straight

  • "Is this a multi-agent framework?" No — it's a product: an open-source, extensible service you self-host. Nothing to assemble, nothing to wire. It runs.
  • "Why not Mem0 / Letta / a memory API?" Memory alone is a database. The bet here is the integration: memory + miners + one consistent execution discipline, shipped together — the way Office shipped together. And the vault stays in your git.
  • "Too early?" The scoreboard is public: six lanes live today, five more in flight. The vault is plain markdown — betting early costs nothing to exit.
