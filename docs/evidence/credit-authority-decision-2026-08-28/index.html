Architecture decision · 2026-08-28

One credit authority.
Local service wallets.

Zenod and Phylax stay independent services. A customer has one commercial balance for the product they bought. Each service keeps its own exact cost ledger, but a paired Phylax wallet can only spend credit explicitly allocated by the product authority.

Status: binding architecture decision Scope: credit authority only No runtime or deployment change Signup remains closed
Recommended decision

For any purchased product perimeter, there is exactly one commercial credit authority. Service-local ledgers are bounded wallets and cost books—not competing customer balances.

In Zenod Hosted, Zenod is the authority and funds its paired Phylax wallet. In Phylax standalone, Phylax is both authority and service. The Phylax runtime and ledger remain the same in both modes.

Paired mode

The customer bought Zenod or PM.

  • Authority: the purchased product.
  • Phylax: one fixed adapter and a bounded wallet funded by that authority.
  • Customer UX: one balance, one plan, one settings experience.
  • Operator truth: separate service costs remain visible for P&L.

Standalone mode

The customer bought Phylax itself.

  • Authority: Phylax billing or a future wallet authority.
  • Phylax: the same local wallet, usage ledger and enforcement.
  • Customer UX: Phylax shows its own single balance.
  • Downstream: one explicitly configured compatible MCP service.
Exact transaction

Reserve locally; replenish occasionally

Check the local allocation.

Before paid work, Phylax checks the spendable units already allocated to its wallet.

Refill on low water or insufficient job balance.

The authenticated controller—not the LLM—asks the authority for a small target tranche when the wallet falls below its low-water mark or when a valid job needs more units than are locally available. Every request carries a stable idempotency key.

Move, never mint.

The authority atomically moves credit from the customer's free pocket into the Phylax escrow pocket. Free balance drops immediately, preventing double spend.

Reserve → work → settle.

Phylax reserves estimated units, performs the channel/STT work, settles actual units, and releases any difference.

Reconcile by immutable IDs.

Usage reports and grants are append-only and idempotent. A lost response replays the same operation; it never creates new credit.

Return unused credit when needed.

Disconnect, cancellation, expiry or product closure can return unspent escrow through an explicit idempotent transfer.

Authority versus accounting

Two ledgers do not have to mean two authorities

Responsibility Zenod authority Phylax local ledger
Can create customer spendable credit Yes No
Can allocate a bounded service tranche Yes Only accepts an exact grant
Admits real-time Phylax work Not per message Yes, within allocation
Records Phylax service cost Receives reconciled totals Exact append-only events
Customer-facing balance One combined authority projection Hidden service detail
Can overspend the customer account No No; allocation is a hard ceiling
The small tranche is the maximum temporary reconciliation exposure. It is not a second subscription or a second customer-owned balance. The authority can conservatively stop refills at any time without interrupting already-funded work.
Exact-or-refuse: the controller requests enough to cover the valid job and restore the configured target, bounded by the service cap. The authority authenticates the mapped service and checks the master balance before returning either the exact idempotent grant or a typed refusal. There is no partial mystery grant and no minting. Admission retries once after a grant; on refusal, paid work pauses cleanly.
What already exists

We keep the solid pieces

  • Separate Zenod and Phylax services and immutable images.
  • A tenant-scoped Phylax append-only allowance/usage ledger.
  • Idempotent grants and reserve/settle/release paid-work semantics.
  • Zenod-owned product identity, subscription and combined usage view.
  • Service-authenticated management MCP between the two systems.
What becomes clearer

We remove the ambiguity

  • Call Zenod the sole commercial authority in paired mode.
  • Call Phylax credit a service allocation or escrow—not a second customer balance.
  • Refill small tranches when the wallet reaches its low-water mark or a valid job estimate exceeds the locally spendable balance.
  • Expose one customer projection; keep service cost detail operator-only.
  • Make every grant, usage report, return and replay reference immutable operation IDs.
Minimal interface

A backend can change; the contract should not

projection(account)One customer balance and service-allocation breakdown for authorized product/operator views.
ensureAllocation(service, required, target, operationId)Authenticate the mapped service; check the master balance and service cap; then atomically return the exact bounded grant or a typed refusal. Exact replay returns the same outcome.
reportUsage(allocation, eventId, units)Reconcile append-only actual usage without minting or double charging.
returnAllocation(allocation, units, operationId)Return unused service credit to the authority's free pocket.
suspend / resume(account)Stop new refills and paid work consistently across the product perimeter.
Future L2 compatibility

Blockchain can fund the authority later; it should not meter every message

Good L2 use

Wallet top-ups, credit issuance, escrow transfers, withdrawals and periodic settlement anchors.

Bad L2 use

A transaction for every inbound message, transcription second or outbound receipt.

Why

Real-time work must not inherit gas, nonce, finality, reorg, privacy or RPC-availability failure modes.

A future L2CreditAuthority can implement the same allocation contract. Phylax does not change: it still spends a bounded local tranche and reports exact service usage. That makes an L2 migration additive, not another refactor.

Guardrails

What this decision explicitly avoids

  • No shared Zenod/Phylax database.
  • No browser-held service credential.
  • No provider API key treated as credit.
  • No blockchain dependency in the beta path.
  • No remote authorization round-trip for every message.
  • No separate customer subscription inside paired Phylax.
  • No silent balance creation during reconciliation.
  • No change to WhatsApp sessions, routing or media handling.
