1   What the terms say, read on 9 November 2026

Go is for you and your coding agents. DeepSeek is for your customers.

"You will only use the Services for your own internal use, and not on behalf of or for the benefit of any third party."OpenCode terms of service, effective 8 November 2026. "Services" includes its inference product, so Go and Console credit alike.
"OpenCode Go is designed for OpenCode and other coding agents that produce similar types of requests. Traffic is monitored for abuse that degrades the experience for other users."OpenCode Go docs, Where can I use it. Go and Go Plus are for one-member workspaces.
"You can integrate the capabilities of the DeepSeek models into various downstream systems, applications, or functionalities … providing services to both internal and external end users."DeepSeek Open Platform terms, effective 29 April 2026, section 1.1

What Go would have given: an OpenAI-style endpoint at opencode.ai/inference/go with deepseek-v4.1-flash, $100 of V4.1 Flash use a month for $10, capped at $20 per 5 hours and $50 a week. At the limit calls are blocked unless Extra Usage draws on Console credit. That tenfold is the part the terms keep for your own coding. Your Go key still fits the team's own test runs, which is your own use.

2   How it is set up

Background jobs go to DeepSeek. What a user waits for stays on OpenRouter.

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, own accountFiling a note, desk jobs, persona runs and, once it exists, nightly self-reflection. Each job type has its own switch.
⇄
OpenRouter, as todayChat answers, deeper questions and image reading. Calls stay on the OpenAI key. It also takes over any background call DeepSeek fails.
  • Nothing lost. If DeepSeek errors, the same call runs on OpenRouter. If the balance runs out, DeepSeek is skipped for 10 minutes at a time and work carries on.
  • Caps unchanged. Every DeepSeek call is checked against and counted in the same monthly AI allowance and feature caps.
  • Cost per provider. Each call is logged with its provider and its real DeepSeek charge, peak hours included, so the usage log splits DeepSeek from OpenRouter.
  • Balance alert. The health check reads the DeepSeek balance every 15 minutes and shows low under $5 and empty at $0, with one alert line in the logs.

5   What you do, the switch-over and the way back

One account, $10 and one key. We do the rest.

  1. You: open a DeepSeek account and top up $10 at platform.deepseek.com, instead of $10 on Go or OpenRouter. Then create an API key.
  2. You: paste the key into the private secret card we send. It lands as DEEPSEEK_API_KEY on production only, never in chat or the repo.
  3. We: redeploy and watch. The health check turns from off to ok with the balance, the first filings show up under DeepSeek in the usage log, and we compare a week of real costs against this page.
  4. Way back: remove the key, or set one job back to OpenRouter, and redeploy. Everything returns to today's route. No data moves either way.

Keep the Go plan for your own coding and the team's test runs. If you want Go for customers anyway, the only clean way is to ask OpenCode in writing first. Nothing was bought and no model was called for this page.
