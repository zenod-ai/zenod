# How Zenod should send tester mail

Research for Jordi's question on 10 November 2026: "Why are we doing a Resend account? Were we not using our own infrastructure? I don't want to use Resend. It's expensive. I don't like it. What are people doing for this purpose?"

## Bottom line

**Send from Google Workspace, the one that already runs zenod.dev mail.** It costs nothing extra, and Gmail delivers well. Mail goes out as **jordi@zenod.dev**, so testers' replies land in your inbox. Zenod gets its own Google key, separate from the team's, with one permission: send mail. It cannot read mail.

You were right to push back. We already have our own mail, and asking for Resend was a mistake. To be fair to Resend, its free plan would have cost nothing at our volume, which is tens of emails a month. It is still a new outside account we don't need.

One sitting with Claude on your Mac, about 15 minutes, sets it up. Agents do the rest and send the first test to you before any tester gets one.

## What we checked today

- **zenod.dev is on your Google Workspace.** Its mail goes to Google, and its sender record already names Google. The team's Google key was allowed to act for jordi@zenod.dev today, in the same Workspace as alpha9.io. It was refused for support@zenod.dev, which is not a mailbox of its own.
- **zenod.dev has no Google signature record yet.** The usual name for that record is empty, so Gmail's signing for zenod.dev is not switched on. Mail still arrives without it, but more of it lands in spam. Switching it on is one page in Google admin plus one record in Cloudflare.
- **Gmail's limit is far above our needs.** Each Workspace user can send 2,000 messages a day. Zenod needs tens a month.
- **Our servers cannot send mail directly.** Hetzner blocks the outgoing mail port on both of our servers, and we tested both today. Hetzner opens it only on request, case by case. The port that Gmail and the other services use for sending is open.
- **Zenod keeps its own key.** Zenod is standalone. It never calls the team's systems while running and never borrows the team's key, so it needs a key of its own. The team's mail key stays with the team.

## The options

At our volume, tens a month and a few hundred at most:

| Option | Cost a month | Who must act | Gets to the inbox | Our own? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Google Workspace, Zenod's own key** | €0, already paid | You once, with Claude on your Mac, about 15 min. Agents do the rest | Very good once signing is on | Yes, your domain and Google account |
| Google Workspace relay, our server allowed by address | €0 | You once in Google admin, about 5 min | Very good | Yes, but every program on that server could then send as any of your addresses |
| Our own mail server on Hetzner | €0 in money, a lot of upkeep | You ask Hetzner to open the port, which they may refuse. Agents run the server | Poor at first, because Hetzner addresses often land in spam | Fully, but it is the hard way |
| Cloudflare email sending | about $5, its paid Workers plan | Agents, plus your yes to the paid plan | Good | Partly, our DNS is already there. Still in public beta |
| Amazon SES | under $0.10, about $0.10 to $0.16 per thousand emails | You open an AWS account with a card and ask Amazon to lift its trial limit | Good | No, a new outside account |
| Resend | €0 on the free plan, up to 100 a day | You open an account | Good | No, a new outside account |

What people usually do: small products mostly use a sending service like Resend, Postmark or Amazon SES. People who already pay for Google Workspace often just send through it when volume is low, which is our case. Few run their own mail server now, because getting into inboxes from a cloud server takes months of care.

## Why the Workspace route

- It is ours and already paid for. No new account, no new bill.
- Mail comes from you at zenod.dev and replies come to you. That fits a small group of testers.
- Google's own servers deliver well. Hetzner's blocked port does not matter, because Zenod hands the mail to Google.
- Zenod's key sits only in Zenod's production settings. It can send and cannot read mail. Rollback is one click: remove its line in Google admin and the key stops working.
- One honest limit: Google lets a send-only key send as any address in your Workspace, not just jordi@zenod.dev. Zenod's code will name jordi@zenod.dev as the only sender, and the key never leaves Zenod's production settings. A narrower option exists, a password for that one mailbox, but that password can also read the mailbox, so it is worse.

## Proposed ask E26-A6 (not posted, for the Delivery Manager to post)

**E26-A6 · needs Mac agent · about 15 minutes**

Let Claude on your Mac set up Zenod's own mail through your Google Workspace, so Zenod can email each accepted tester their link from jordi@zenod.dev.

- **Why it matters:** testers are accepted automatically now, but nobody gets their link until Zenod can send mail.
- **Recommend:** yes. It's free, uses your own Google account and domain, and needs no new account.
- **Impact:** each accepted tester gets their link within minutes, and the welcome email can follow. Replies come to you.
- **Risk:** low. The key can only send, and it stays in Zenod's production settings. Rollback is one click: remove its line in Google admin.
- **Why you:** only your Google login can create the key and allow it.

Steps for Claude on your Mac (it signs in as you and asks before each click):

1. In Google Cloud, open Zenod's own project, the one that holds Zenod's Google Drive sign-in. Create a service account named zenod-mail with no roles. You press Create key yourself, and you save the key file in the private file box on the steps page. Claude never opens or reads it.
2. In Google admin, open domain-wide delegation and add zenod-mail's Client ID with one permission only, sending mail. You press Authorize.
3. In Google admin, open Gmail and then Authenticate email. Pick zenod.dev and generate a record. Claude adds it to zenod.dev in your Cloudflare and presses Start authentication once Google sees it.
4. In Google admin Users, check that jordi@zenod.dev is a real mailbox and not only an alias, and note which one it is.

Then agents put the key into Zenod's production settings, build the sender and send a test to you before any tester.

Bootstrap text for Claude on your Mac, delivered as the steps page and laptop ticket:

> You are Claude on Jordi's Mac, working a Zenod ticket. Find the Zenod repository from github.com/zenod-ai/zenod and read ask E26-A6 in docs/tester-invite-three-clear-promises-first.md. Do its four steps in Jordi's own Chrome while he watches. He presses Create key, Authorize and every terms box himself. Never open, read or move the key file: Jordi saves it in the private file box on the steps page. Add one mail permission only, and never change any other project, key, delegation line or domain. Then mark each step Done or Stuck on the steps page, with a note that names the project, zenod-mail's Client ID, and whether jordi@zenod.dev is a mailbox or an alias. Never include the key.
