Each run re-derives the roots from scratch, so a name or grouping you liked can drift.

Steering

Root summaries and closure

Five rules keep the tree recognisable week to week

Stable identity

Each root has an id. Next week's cluster inherits the id and name when it shares at least half its pages with this week's root (Jaccard ≥ 0.5). The name only changes when the topic really changed.

Change budget

At most 3 structural changes a week (promote, close, merge, split, rename). Doing nothing is the default.

Hysteresis

A topic is promoted at heat 0.9 and demoted below 0.3. Merges and splits need two weeks in a row of the same signal. Built for roots in E9-2g; now applied to every change.

Your words win

Names come from your page titles and your own captures, never from agent reports. A pin, a rename or an alias you give is never undone by the keeper.

Evidence before structure

A root needs at least 3 pages or a week of your captures. Below that, pages wait under "Not yet a topic", which is honest and costs nothing.

The test

We replay your last 8 weeks of captures on a copy of the vault. Week to week, at least 80% of roots keep their id and name, and you recognise the final tree.

Borrowed from incremental clustering practice (Jaccard / Hungarian matching of clusters across runs)9 / 18

The memory-keeper: one persona, two wakes, a hard budget

Daily light wake: about 02:00, $0

  • Update heat from yesterday's own captures
  • Refresh the index for changed pages
  • Queue any provisional leaves for the weekly wake
  • No structural change, no model call

Weekly digestion: Sunday, ≤ $0.05

  • Re-derive topics and heat (no model call)
  • Match them to last week's roots (stable ids)
  • Apply your steering notes first
  • Pick at most 3 changes; doing nothing is the default
  • Model calls only for roots whose pages changed: check the name, rewrite the "Now" line
  • One commit, plus one line in "Recent changes" on Tree.md

Also wakes early when…

Ten or more of your own captures land on pages with no topic. That is the Generative Agents "reflect when enough has piled up" trigger, still capped at one digestion a day.

Who it is in agent_team terms

The Delivery Manager pattern, scoped to one vault. It reconciles (heat and evidence), advances (the few changes), raises one question when it is unsure, and reports (Recent changes). It is the single writer of Tree.md, like the steward of a spine.

Runs where

It runs on the existing durable task lane, beside the weekly sweep, under the vault write lock. It is metered against the tenant's $3 allowance like every other model call.

What it never does

  • rewrite your own words on a page
  • override a pin, a locked name or a steering note
  • count agent reports as your activity
  • make more than 3 structural changes a week
  • send your notes to a provider beyond the metered model calls for names and "Now" lines

What it asks

One question a week at most, and only when two changes are near a tie. For example: "Is Altamira2050 part of Zenod or its own thing?"

Same human-in-the-loop line as the Librarian Doctrine: autopilot for small changes, a proposal for anything a revert can't undo11 / 18

Tier 0: a free keyword index that returns where things live

One SQLite file per user, derived from the vault and rebuildable at any time. SQLite is already in Zenod (node:sqlite).

What goes in

  • one row per page: title, aliases, lineage path, summary, "Now" line, body
  • one row per Log entry: date, source, first line, text, and the page it was filed: on
  • root rows too, so a question can land on a whole topic (RAPTOR's "collapsed tree")

How it ranks

  • FTS5 bm25() with weights: title and aliases 10, path 5, summary 3, body 1
  • tokenizer unicode61 remove_diacritics 2, for Spanish and English, no stemming
  • the lineage is put in front of every row's text ("Working › House › Visits"), a free version of Anthropic's contextual BM25

What you get back

search_memory "notario" → 5 hits in milliseconds, each with its path and its Log anchor. No model call, $0, the same answer every time.

How it stays fresh

The librarian updates the index row for each page it writes. The daily wake checks it against the vault's git head, and a full rebuild is one command.

Why not embeddings yet

Under about 1,000 notes, keyword search with one author finds the right note most of the time (Librarian Doctrine). Embeddings come later as a reranker on top, never instead.

Answer to "is it expensive?"

No. The structure work is deterministic and free. Only naming and summarising use a model, and only for what changed: under 10 cents per user per month, at most 7% of the $3 allowance.

The lesson from GraphRAG

Summarising every cluster up front is what makes GraphRAG costly. LazyGraphRAG reports indexing at 0.1% of that cost by deferring summaries. We do the same: summarise only what changed.

"≤ $0.002 per keeper call" is set above a full 47k-token ask call ($0.00133), so it is a ceiling, not a guess14 / 18

One question for you: build it as proposed?

A yes takes these three defaults. Change any of them in one word.

1 · How free is the keeper? Default: acts within a budget

It makes up to 3 revertable changes a week on its own and shows each one under "Recent changes". You undo any with "that's wrong". Other options: proposals only (you approve each change), or no budget at all.

2 · Cadence and money? Default: daily $0, weekly ≤ $0.05

A deterministic daily wake and an LLM-assisted weekly digestion, capped at about $0.22 per user per month (7% of the $3 allowance). Option: nightly digestion, at about 7× the model cost.

3 · Where does the index live? Default: beside the vault

A derived SQLite file in your tenant's data directory, not committed to the vault. It is rebuildable, so your vault holds only your notes. Option: commit it into the vault.

If yes

We record rows E9-5a…5f as ready, start with the index and stable ids, and replay your last 8 weeks before anything touches your real vault.

Risk and undo

Every keeper change is one vault commit, and the backup and revert routine is the same as E9-2g and 2h. The index is derived and can be deleted at any time.
