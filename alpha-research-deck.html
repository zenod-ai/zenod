Thesis

Alpha = information asymmetry.
For the first time, revenue is public and verified.

Historically you guessed what worked from launch posts and vanity metrics. Now platforms like TrustMRR pipe real Stripe/RevenueCat readings, updated hourly — MRR, MoM growth, marketing channels, tech stack, even asking prices. That's a live order book for micro-software.

Old game

Ideate in the dark → build → launch → pray. Signal was noise: upvotes, Twitter hype, unverifiable "MRR screenshots."

New game

Read verified revenue + growth rates → identify niches with proven willingness-to-pay → replicate with a differentiated wedge → out-execute on distribution.

Your edge

You're strong at dev + infra and can run marketing. The bottleneck is picking right. That's what the signal layer solves.

The Signal Stack

Where to read performance before you build

TrustMRR — primary

Stripe/RevenueCat/Superwall-verified MRR + MoM growth, updated hourly. Also exposes marketing channels, tech stack, category pages, a for-sale marketplace with multiples, and a public API. The cleanest demand signal that exists.

Acquire.com & Flippa — pricing signal

Acquire: 500k+ buyers, 2,000+ closed deals, verified financials. Flippa: SaaS deal volume up ~73% in 2025. Asking multiples tell you which niches buyers value (and which sellers are fleeing).

App stores & RevenueCat

App Store / Play top-grossing by category, RevenueCat "State of Subscription Apps" benchmarks. Good for consumer/mobile signal (e.g. Poket photobooth, Quran Daily on TrustMRR).

Builder feeds — leading indicators

Indie Hackers milestones, X build-in-public, TrustMRR feed, Product Hunt. Noisier, but 3–6 months ahead of leaderboards. Watch what repeat winners (Marc Lou, John Rush, Nevo David) ship next.

Rule: never trust one source. Triangulate: verified MRR (TrustMRR) × acquisition multiples (Acquire/Flippa) × momentum (feeds). A niche confirmed by all three is real.
Method

A screening system, not vibes

  1. Momentum screen. Sort TrustMRR by MoM growth with MRR ≥ $20k. Sustained double-digit MoM at scale = market pull, not launch spike.
  2. Cluster detection. One winner is luck; three or more independent products growing in the same niche is a wave. Name the cluster.
  3. Multiple check. Cross-reference for-sale listings. Low multiples (<2x annual) in a growing niche = churny or operator-dependent — a warning about the business model, not just the seller.
  4. Moat audit. Why does the incumbent win — distribution, data, or just being first? If it's "first," a better wedge can take share. If it's data/network, don't fight head-on.
  5. Wedge design. Never clone. Enter with one dimension the leader can't easily copy: a vertical, a workflow, a pricing model, or (best) an autonomy level.
  6. Distribution-first build. 4–8 week MVP, but the marketing engine (SEO/AEO, build-in-public, communities) starts on day 1 — that's the actual product launch.

Why agents beat apps (for you)

Sell the outcome, not the tool

$49 → $2k+

A tool prices like software. An agent prices like headcount: SMB vertical-agent tiers run $500–$2,000/mo; agency-replacement pricing is 30–50% of the manual cost replaced.

45%

YoY growth of the AI agents market ($7.6B → ~$10.9B in 2026), with vertical agents the fastest segment (~63% CAGR). a16z: 30–40% of the ~$450B vertical SaaS market reshaped by agents through 2028.

Moat = memory

Schedulers are commodities (Postiz is literally open source). An agent that accumulates client-specific memory and learns from feedback gets better with tenure — that's switching cost no scheduler has.

The shift already visible in the data: solo operators running AI-powered "agencies" charge $3k+/mo for outcomes that tools sell for $49/mo. Agents let you capture agency pricing with software margins. That's the trade you want.

Bet 1 · Airbnb yield analytics

Real prize, wrong foundation (as scoped)

What's true

High-ticket demand is proven — AirDNA runs enterprise contracts from ~$50k/yr, and a whole ecosystem (Mashvisor, Airbtics, AirROI, Rabbu, PriceLabs) lives off STR data. Your AI angle (yield agent, not dashboard) is a real wedge.

What kills momentum

Supply side: courts have leaned toward public-data scraping being legal, but pipelines are fragile, break on frontend changes, and quality varies wildly. Airbnb's Partner API is effectively closed to unsolicited applicants in 2026. You'd spend months on plumbing before earning $1.

De-risk path A — buy the data

Build on a data API (AirROI, Airbtics, Mashvisor) instead of scraping. Margin compresses but time-to-market collapses; you compete on the AI/agent layer, which is your strength anyway.

De-risk path B — flip the supply side

Get data from hosts/PMs directly (channel-manager integrations, host-connected accounts) — the AirDNA partner-data model. Slower, but proprietary and legally clean. Works if you land 2–3 property managers as design partners.

Verdict: park or de-risk — don't grind. Keep it as the slow, high-ticket bet only if path A or B is viable within ~4 weeks of effort. Do not build a scraper farm.
Bet 2 · Zenod & Council

Every 2026 review of "AI social media agents" reaches the same conclusion: the category is full of AI-assisted schedulers wearing an agent costume — none are truly autonomous. Meanwhile Postiz grew $20k → $157k MRR in months. Demand is proven; autonomy is the open slot.

Memory system

Client feeds a growing knowledge base. This is the moat: posts sourced from their knowledge, not generic AI slop. Value compounds with tenure — churn resistance schedulers can't match.

Three-role team

Memory curator · posting rhythm operator · feedback analyst. Framing it as a hireable team (not a tool) moves you from $49/mo scheduler pricing to $500–$3k/mo agency-replacement pricing.

Feedback loop

Audience reactions flow back into memory. Nobody in the category closes this loop today — it's your "gets better every week" demo moment.

Position against Postiz, not beside it: "Postiz schedules your posts. Zenod is the team that knows your company and posts for you." Sell outcomes (rhythm, engagement, share of voice) — never features.
